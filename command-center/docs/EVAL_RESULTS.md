## Iteration history (honest record of changes made after seeing results)

- **Run 1** (first eval run, same day): Money Detective caught 3 of 6 planted duplicates. All three misses were copies of monthly rent bills, which the old rule ignored ("same amount repeated more than 3 times within 30 days is a pattern"). On the six unmodified companies it raised 7 duplicate flags (subcontract installment series at company-024). The report also counted 2 "customers paying later than usual" findings as false positives because their id started with `unusual-`; that was a scoring bug. Intent extraction missed one holdout field: the reserve in "never dropping below $100,000" (`ho-conflict-hire-reserve`).
- **Change after run 1**: the duplicate rule now flags identical bills ≤3 days apart only if the gap is under half the vendor's usual billing interval, or the amount is rare for that vendor. The late-customer finding was renamed (`late-customers`). We did **not** change the parser for the holdout miss: holdout failures stay as they are.
- **Run 2**: 6 of 6 planted duplicates and 6 of 6 planted spikes caught, 1 flag on the six unmodified companies. We then scanned **all 100** unmodified companies: 33 companies flagged, 111 duplicate/unusual flags, almost all in project-based companies (job-costed subcontract installments, materials, equipment rental).
- **Change after run 2**: job-costed bills are compared only with bills on the same project, and a project installment counts as a duplicate only if its amount is rare. Amounts within 1% count as "the same" (installment series differ by a cent).
- **Run 3** (the tables above). The remaining flags on clean data are mostly large but legitimate purchase orders at supply-and-install companies, plus repeated equipment-rental and estimating-service charges on projects. We stopped tuning here and report them as false positives.

## How each metric is computed

- **Company match**: only cases with no company selected. Correct = the expected company, or a clarifying question when the prompt is ambiguous.
- **Intent extraction**: each expected field in `eval/cases.ts` (decision type, question kind, hire count, salary, draw, marketing, price, equipment, stress flags, delay days, reserve, horizon, counterfactual customer) compared exactly with the validated task.
- **Required records**: the record types the task needs ⊆ the record types retrieved.
- **Calculation correctness**: every report-verifiable number the system shows (cash, receivables, overdue receivables, payables, annual profit, change in cash, annual revenue) compared to the dataset's own balance sheet, aging reports and P&L, to the cent. For the plain LLM, to within $1.
- **Feasibility**: for decision cases, whether the system's verdict (a feasible plan exists or not) matches the expectation. Each recommendation it calls feasible is replayed through fresh projections to check the reserve (base case) and solvency (payment-delay stress). The "conflicting" cases cannot be satisfied, so the correct answer is to say so.
- **Latency**: in-process wall time of the full pipeline per case (the same clock as the Sentry spans), after one warm-up run, with caches cleared before each case. HTTP and browser time are excluded. Plain-LLM latency is one model call.
- **Cost**: tokens from `usageMetadata` × list price per million tokens. The price is used only when configured (`GEMINI_PRICE_IN_PER_M` / `GEMINI_PRICE_OUT_PER_M`) or recorded in `src/lib/pipeline/cost.ts` for the model. Otherwise the report says "unknown price" and shows tokens.
